## WELCOME & HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

If you've recently received your child's autism diagnosis — or if you've had the diagnosis for years and still feel like you're missing something — this guide is for you.

The United States has a patchwork of programs, benefits, and funding sources designed to support families like yours. The problem is that no one hands you a roadmap. Programs are administered by different agencies, have different eligibility rules, and use different jargon. Families often miss out on thousands of dollars per year simply because they didn't know to ask.

This guide changes that.

- Exactly how to apply, what documents you need, and what to say

## SECTION 1: THE BIG PICTURE — WHAT YOUR FAMILY IS ENTITLED TO

### The Four Categories of Support

Every benefit available to your family falls into one of four buckets:

**1. Federal Entitlement Programs** — Programs where if you meet the criteria, you ARE entitled to the benefit by law. No lottery, no waiting list.
- Supplemental Security Income (SSI)
- Medicaid (base program)
- IDEA Early Intervention and Special Education

**2. State Waiver Programs** — Medicaid waivers that fund intensive support services. These often have waiting lists, but the services can be life-changing.
- Home and Community Based Services (HCBS) waivers
- Autism-specific waivers (in some states)

**3. Federal Assistance Programs** — Income-based support programs your family may qualify for.
- SNAP (food assistance)
- WIC (for children under 5)
- Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8)
- LIHEAP (energy assistance)

**4. Savings & Asset Programs** — Special accounts that protect your child's financial future.
- ABLE Accounts
- Special Needs Trusts

### Quick Eligibility Snapshot

| Benefit | Age Limit | Income Test | Disability Requirement | Waiting List? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SSI | Under 18 | Yes (family income) | Yes | No |
| Medicaid | Under 19 (base) | Yes | Yes (with SSI) | No |
| HCBS Waivers | Varies by state | No (usually) | Yes | Often |
| Early Intervention | Birth to 3 | No | Yes | No |
| Special Education (IDEA) | 3–21 | No | Yes | No |
| SNAP | All ages | Yes | No | No |
| ABLE Account | Under 46 at diagnosis | No | Yes | No |

## SECTION 2: SUPPLEMENTAL SECURITY INCOME (SSI)

### What Is SSI?
SSI is a federal cash benefit program run by the Social Security Administration. For children with autism, it can provide up to **$943/month** (2024 rate, adjusted annually) to help cover the costs of care.

More importantly: **qualifying for SSI usually automatically qualifies your child for Medicaid**, which covers therapies, medical equipment, and services worth tens of thousands of dollars per year.

### Does My Child Qualify?

Your child must meet **both** tests:

**Test 1: Disability Test**
Your child must have a medically determinable physical or mental impairment that causes marked and severe functional limitations. Autism typically qualifies, but the SSA evaluates *how* autism affects your child's daily functioning.

The SSA evaluates six domains of functioning:
1. Acquiring and using information
2. Attending and completing tasks
3. Interacting and relating with others
4. Moving about and manipulating objects
5. Caring for yourself
6. Health and physical well-being

To qualify, your child must have either:
- An "extreme" limitation in ONE domain, OR
- "Marked" limitations in TWO domains

**Test 2: Financial Test (For Children Under 18)**
For children under 18, the SSA counts part of the parents' income in what's called "deeming." This means high household income can disqualify your child even with a severe disability.

**2024 Income Limits (Approximate — Varies by Situation):**
- 1 parent in household, 1 child with disability: ~$4,400/month gross income
- 2 parents in household, 1 child with disability: ~$5,500/month gross income
- Add ~$400 per additional child in the household

*Note: Certain income and assets are excluded from this calculation — more details below.*

**Important:** When your child turns 18, parental income is NO LONGER counted. Almost every young adult with autism should apply for SSI on their 18th birthday, regardless of family income during childhood.

### What Counts as Income (And What Doesn't)

**Counted as income:**
- Wages, salary, self-employment income
- Unemployment benefits
- Alimony and child support
- Most pension/retirement income

**Excluded from counting:**
- The first $20/month of any income
- Food stamps (SNAP)
- Home energy assistance (LIHEAP)
- Income of other children in the household
- Student loan money used for education expenses
- Certain housing assistance

**What counts as assets:**
- Cash, checking/savings accounts
- Stocks, bonds, investments
- Property (other than your primary home)
- Vehicles (over the first one)

**Asset limit:** $2,000 for the child individually. However, **ABLE accounts** (covered later) allow you to save up to $100,000 without affecting SSI eligibility.

### How to Apply

**Step 1: Gather documents**
- Child's birth certificate
- Child's Social Security card
- Proof of citizenship or immigration status
- Child's medical records and diagnosis documentation
- Recent school records (IEP, evaluation reports)
- Proof of your household income and assets (tax returns, pay stubs, bank statements)
- Names and contact info of all treating doctors, therapists, schools

**Step 2: Apply**
- **Online:** SSA.gov/benefits/ssi (fastest for most families)
- **Phone:** Call 1-800-772-1213 (M–F, 8am–7pm ET)
- **In person:** Locate your local Social Security office at SSA.gov/locator

**Step 3: The Child Function Report**
After applying, you'll receive a Child Function Report (SSA-3375) to complete. This is CRITICAL — it's where you describe how autism affects your child's daily functioning.

**How to complete the Child Function Report effectively:**
- Describe your child's WORST days, not typical days
- Be specific and concrete: "My child cannot...", "requires constant..."
- Use the six functional domains as your framework
- Don't minimize or "put your best foot forward" — this is not the time to emphasize positives
- Describe what you have to do to help your child function (the caregiver burden)
- Example strong statement: "On bad days, my 8-year-old cannot dress himself, requires physical prompting to eat, and cannot be left unsupervised for any length of time due to self-injurious behavior."

**Step 4: Medical evaluation**
The SSA may request a consultative examination. Attend it, bring documentation, and describe your child's functioning accurately to the examiner.

### What If You're Denied?

**DO NOT give up.** Approximately 60% of initial SSI claims are denied, and roughly 60% of appealed decisions are overturned.

**Appeal deadlines are strict — you have 60 days from denial to appeal.**

Appeal levels:
1. **Reconsideration** — Request a new review of your case
2. **Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) Hearing** — Present your case before a judge
3. **Appeals Council** — Request review of the ALJ decision
4. **Federal Court** — Last resort

At the ALJ level, consider hiring a disability attorney or advocate. Most work on contingency (no fee unless you win, and fees are capped by law at 25% of back pay, max $7,200).

**Resources:**
- National Organization of Social Security Claimants' Representatives: nosscr.org
- Your state's legal aid organization (often free representation)

## SECTION 3: MEDICAID

### The Two Paths to Medicaid

**Path 1: SSI-Linked Medicaid**
In most states, if your child receives SSI, they automatically get Medicaid. This is typically the most comprehensive coverage.

**Path 2: CHIP or State Medicaid Programs**
Even without SSI, your child may qualify for Medicaid or CHIP (Children's Health Insurance Program) based on family income.

**2024 CHIP Income Limits (vary significantly by state):**
- Many states cover children up to 200–300% of the Federal Poverty Level
- Some states (like California, New York) cover up to 400%+
- At 300% FPL: ~$90,000/year for a family of 4 qualifies

Check your state: healthcare.gov/medicaid-chip/children's-health-insurance-program

### What Medicaid Covers for Autism

When properly enrolled, Medicaid must cover **"medically necessary" treatment** for children under 21. Under a provision called EPSDT (Early and Periodic Screening, Diagnostic and Treatment), states must cover:

- **ABA therapy** (Applied Behavior Analysis) — typically 20–40 hours/week
- Speech therapy
- Occupational therapy
- Physical therapy
- Psychological evaluations and therapy
- Medical devices and equipment
- Prescription medications
- Mental health services
- Dental care
- Vision care

**Key:** The word "medically necessary" is powerful. Insurers and Medicaid agencies often deny claims claiming something isn't medically necessary. This is frequently wrong and can be appealed.

### Medicaid Waivers (HCBS Waivers)

This is where the real money is — but most families don't know about it.

Medicaid Home and Community Based Services (HCBS) waivers allow states to cover services that traditional Medicaid doesn't cover, including:
- **Respite care** (giving caregivers a break) — 30+ hours/month in some states
- **Day programs and habilitation**
- **Supported employment**
- **Assistive technology**
- **Home modifications** (ramps, sensory rooms)
- **Transportation**
- **Behavioral support services** beyond what school provides

**Average annual value of HCBS waiver services: $20,000–$80,000+ per year**

**The catch:** Many states have waiting lists of 5–10+ years.

**What you must do TODAY:** Get on your state's waiver waiting list as soon as possible. The list is first-come, first-served. Many families wait years and don't start the process until their child is a teenager — this is a critical mistake.

### How to Find and Apply for Waivers in Your State

1. Visit your state's Medicaid agency website
2. Search for "[Your State] Medicaid waiver autism" or "[Your State] HCBS waiver developmental disabilities"
3. Call your state's Developmental Disabilities agency directly
4. Contact your local Regional Center (in California) or equivalent agency

**Resources:**
- KFF Medicaid Waiver Tracker: kff.org/medicaid/issue-brief/medicaid-home-and-community-based-services
- Autism Speaks State Resource Guide: autismspeaks.org/tool-kit/state-resource-guide

## SECTION 4: EARLY INTERVENTION (BIRTH TO AGE 3)

### Federal Law Guarantees Free Services

Under IDEA Part C (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act), every child from birth to age 3 who has a developmental delay or disability is entitled to free early intervention services.

**This means:**
- Free developmental evaluation
- Free therapy services (speech, OT, PT, developmental specialist)
- Services provided in the child's "natural environment" (usually your home)
- An Individualized Family Service Plan (IFSP) — the precursor to an IEP

### How to Access Early Intervention

1. Call your state's Early Intervention program (each state has one)
2. Request a free evaluation — they have 45 days to complete it
3. If eligible, services begin within 30 days of the IFSP meeting
4. Services continue until the child's 3rd birthday

**To find your state's program:** Search "[State] Early Intervention" or call the RAISE Center: 1-800-695-0285

### Critical Note on Age 3 Transition
At age 3, Early Intervention ends and children transition to the school district for special education services. This transition must be planned — a meeting should happen around your child's 2.5 birthday to begin the IEP process. **Do not let this transition happen without a plan.**

## SECTION 5: SPECIAL EDUCATION UNDER IDEA

### Your Child Has Federal Legal Rights

Under IDEA Part B (for ages 3–21), your child with autism has the legal right to:
- A **Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE)**
- Education in the **Least Restrictive Environment (LRE)**
- An **Individualized Education Program (IEP)** that meets their unique needs
- Related services (speech, OT, PT, counseling, transportation)
- Protection from discrimination

**Cost to you: $0. These services are federally mandated and 100% free.**

The full IEP process is covered in the companion guide "IEP Mastery: The Parent's Advocacy Bible." Key points:

- You have the right to request an evaluation at any time
- You have the right to disagree with the school's evaluation (and get an independent evaluation at the district's expense)
- You have the right to bring an advocate to any IEP meeting
- Schools cannot change a child's placement without parental consent

## SECTION 6: SNAP (FOOD ASSISTANCE)

### Does Your Family Qualify?

SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) eligibility is based on household income and size.

**2024 Gross Monthly Income Limits:**
| Household Size | Monthly Gross Income Limit |
|---|---|
| 1 | $1,580 |
| 2 | $2,137 |
| 3 | $2,694 |
| 4 | $3,250 |
| 5 | $3,807 |
| Each additional person | +$557 |

*Note: Households with a member receiving SSI or certain other benefits may qualify under different rules.*

**Average SNAP benefit: ~$230/month per person**

### How to Apply
Apply online through your state's SNAP website (search "[State] SNAP apply online") or at your local Department of Social Services.

### SNAP and Autism-Specific Expenses
In some states, households with a disabled member may deduct excess medical expenses (including therapy costs not covered by insurance) from their income for SNAP purposes. Ask about the **"medical deduction"** when you apply.

## SECTION 7: WIC (WOMEN, INFANTS AND CHILDREN)

For children under 5 with autism, WIC provides:
- Specific approved foods (tailored for nutritional needs)
- Formula support for children with feeding challenges
- Nutrition counseling
- Referrals to other services

**Income eligibility:** 185% of federal poverty level
**Apply:** Through your local WIC office (search "[State] WIC apply")

## SECTION 8: HOUSING ASSISTANCE

### Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher
This federal program subsidizes housing costs for low-income families. Vouchers cover the gap between a "reasonable market rent" and 30% of the family's income.

**Wait for it:** Section 8 waitlists are notoriously long (often 2–5+ years). Apply now, even if you don't currently need it.

**Apply:** Through your local Public Housing Authority (HUD.gov/info/locators/phalocator)

### Special Needs Housing Programs
Many states have specific housing programs for individuals with disabilities that have separate waitlists or preference points for families with disabled children.

**Search:** "[State] housing assistance developmental disability" or contact your state's DD agency.

## SECTION 9: ABLE ACCOUNTS

### A Game-Changer for Financial Planning

The Achieving a Better Life Experience (ABLE) Act created tax-advantaged savings accounts for people with disabilities. For families of children with autism, this is one of the most powerful tools available.

### Key Benefits

- **Savings without losing SSI:** ABLE account balances up to $100,000 don't count toward the $2,000 SSI asset limit
- **Tax-free growth:** Like a 529 education account, earnings grow tax-free
- **Flexible use:** Funds can be used for housing, education, transportation, healthcare, technology, and more
- **No income test:** Anyone can contribute (family, friends, the person with disability)
- **Annual contribution limit:** $18,000/year (2024) from all sources

### Who Qualifies
- Autism diagnosis before age 46
- Must be receiving SSI/SSDI or have a diagnosis letter from a licensed physician certifying functional limitations

### How to Open
Visit ABLENRC.org to find your state's ABLE program (or choose any state's program — you're not limited to your home state). Accounts typically take 15–30 minutes to open.

**Monthly contribution strategy:** If grandparents, relatives, or anyone wants to give your child money for birthdays/holidays, direct it to the ABLE account. Over 18 years at $200/month, that's $43,000+ (before growth).

## SECTION 10: GRANTS FOR AUTISM FAMILIES

### Federal and State Grants
Several federal and state programs offer grants for autism-specific services. Check:
- **SAMHSA Block Grants:** State-administered, fund behavioral health services
- **Developmental Disabilities Planning Council:** Advocacy and support grants
- **USDA Rural Development:** Housing and community programs for rural families

### Private Foundation Grants
These organizations provide direct financial assistance to autism families:

| Organization | Grant Amount | Focus Area |
|---|---|---|
| Autism Care Today | Up to $1,500/quarter | Therapy, biomedical treatment |
| ACT Today! | Varies | ABA therapy, equipment |
| Doug Flutie Jr. Foundation | Varies | Family support services |
| Organization for Autism Research | Up to $1,000 | Research participation |
| Autism Speaks Family Services | Varies | Crisis + emergency assistance |
| NEXT for AUTISM | Varies | Transition programs |
| The Ellie Fund | Varies | Pediatric care |

**How to maximize grant success:**
1. Apply to multiple grants at the same time
2. Keep applications specific about the need and amount
3. Include documentation from your child's treatment team
4. Follow up 2–3 weeks after submission
5. Reapply after denial — grant committees change

## SECTION 11: TAX BENEFITS FOR AUTISM FAMILIES

### Medical Expense Deduction
If you itemize deductions (rather than taking the standard deduction), you can deduct medical and dental expenses exceeding 7.5% of your adjusted gross income (AGI).

**What counts as a deductible medical expense for autism:**
- ABA therapy (cost not covered by insurance)
- Speech, OT, and PT therapy co-pays and deductibles
- Specialized diets prescribed by a physician (GFCF diet, etc.)
- Sensory integration equipment (when prescribed)
- Special education tuition (in some cases)
- Transportation to and from medical appointments
- Medical conferences on autism (if recommended by doctor)

### Child and Dependent Care Tax Credit
If you pay for child care so you can work, you may claim 20–35% of up to $3,000 in expenses ($6,000 for two or more dependents).

**Autism application:** Day programs, summer programs, and certain respite care may qualify as "dependent care" expenses.

### Dependent Care FSA
If your employer offers a Flexible Spending Account, contribute the maximum ($5,000) to pay for qualifying care expenses with pre-tax dollars — saving you 22–37% on those costs.

## GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS

**ABLE Account:** Tax-advantaged savings account for people with disabilities, doesn't affect SSI eligibility up to $100,000.

**ABA Therapy:** Applied Behavior Analysis — evidence-based treatment for autism.

**CHIP:** Children's Health Insurance Program — low-cost health insurance for children.

**Deeming:** Process by which the SSA counts parent income toward child's SSI eligibility.

**EPSDT:** Early and Periodic Screening, Diagnostic and Treatment — Medicaid provision requiring comprehensive services for children under 21.

**FAPE:** Free Appropriate Public Education — your child's legal right under IDEA.

**HCBS Waiver:** Home and Community Based Services waiver — Medicaid program funding community-based services.

**IDEA:** Individuals with Disabilities Education Act — federal law guaranteeing special education rights.

**IEP:** Individualized Education Program — legal document specifying your child's special education services.

**LRE:** Least Restrictive Environment — legal requirement that children with disabilities be educated alongside non-disabled peers to the maximum extent appropriate.

**Medicaid:** Federal/state health insurance program for low-income individuals.

**SNAP:** Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program — formerly known as food stamps.

**SSI:** Supplemental Security Income — federal cash benefit for low-income individuals with disabilities.

**WIC:** Women, Infants and Children — nutrition assistance program for pregnant women and children under 5.
