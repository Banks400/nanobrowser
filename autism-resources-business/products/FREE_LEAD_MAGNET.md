## PROGRAM #1: SUPPLEMENTAL SECURITY INCOME (SSI)

**What it is:** A federal cash benefit from the Social Security Administration.

**The money:** Up to **$943/month** (2024) for eligible children.

**Why it's powerful:** In most states, qualifying for SSI automatically qualifies your child for Medicaid — which can cover tens of thousands of dollars in ABA therapy, speech, OT, PT, and other services.

**The key rule you need to know:** SSI for children is based partly on parental income. But when your child turns **18**, parental income is NO LONGER counted. Every young adult with autism should apply for SSI on their 18th birthday — even if they were denied as a child.

**Your first step:** Go to SSA.gov to check eligibility and begin an application. Or call 1-800-772-1213.

**Pro tip from our Benefits Navigator:** Many families are denied initially but win on appeal. Don't give up after a first denial — appeal within 60 days.

## PROGRAM #2: HCBS MEDICAID WAIVER

**What it is:** Home and Community Based Services waivers fund intensive support services for people with developmental disabilities.

**The value:** $20,000–$80,000+ per year in funded services, including:
- Respite care (giving caregivers a break)
- Day programs
- Supported employment
- Home modifications
- Assistive technology
- Transportation

**The catch:** Most states have waiting lists of 5–10+ years.

**YOUR MOST IMPORTANT ACTION TODAY:** Call your state's Developmental Disabilities agency and ask to be placed on the HCBS waiver waiting list immediately.

The families who receive these services first are not the families with the most severe needs — they are the families who got in line earliest.

**How to find your state's agency:** Google "[Your State] Medicaid HCBS waiver developmental disabilities" or call 211 (a social services helpline).

## PROGRAM #3: IDEA — FREE SCHOOL SERVICES

**What it is:** The Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) guarantees your child a **free appropriate public education** with all needed services — at no cost to you.

**What this covers:**
- ABA therapy through school
- Speech-Language Therapy
- Occupational Therapy
- Physical Therapy
- Transportation
- Assistive technology
- Social skills services

**Your right:** If your child is between 3 and 21, you can request a free special education evaluation from your school district at any time. They must respond within 60 days.

For children 0–3: Call your state's Early Intervention program — similar services are available for infants and toddlers with developmental delays.

**Key word to remember:** "FAPE" — Free Appropriate Public Education. This is your child's legal right. If the school says they can't provide something, ask: "Then who will provide it as part of FAPE?"

## PROGRAM #4: ABLE ACCOUNTS

**What it is:** A tax-advantaged savings account created specifically for people with disabilities.

**Why it changes the game:**
- Save up to **$100,000** without affecting SSI eligibility
- Contributions grow tax-free
- Can be used for housing, education, technology, healthcare, transportation, and more
- Anyone can contribute — family members, friends, employers

**The $2,000 problem:** People receiving SSI can only have $2,000 in assets before benefits are reduced. For decades, this meant families had to spend down any savings before their child could keep benefits.

ABLE accounts changed this permanently. Up to $100,000 in an ABLE account is invisible to SSI.

**How to open one:** Visit ABLENRC.org — takes about 15 minutes.

**Our recommendation:** If grandparents, relatives, or friends ask what they can give your child for birthdays/holidays, ask them to contribute to the ABLE account instead.

## PROGRAM #5: INSURANCE COVERAGE FOR ABA THERAPY

**What it is:** All 50 states have passed autism insurance reform laws requiring most health insurance plans to cover ABA therapy and other autism treatments.

**What this means:** Your insurance company cannot simply refuse to cover ABA therapy. If they deny coverage, they likely have to justify that denial using specific clinical criteria — and you have the right to appeal.

**Common coverage under these mandates:**
- ABA therapy (often $5,000–$15,000/month in value)
- Speech-language therapy
- Occupational therapy
- Diagnostic evaluations

**Your first steps:**
1. Call the member services number on the back of your insurance card
2. Ask: "What is my plan's coverage for autism spectrum disorder treatment, specifically ABA therapy?"
3. Ask: "What's the process for requesting prior authorization?"
4. Get everything in writing

**If they deny:** You have the right to appeal. Most denials can be overturned with the right documentation. Our Autism Insurance Battle Guide covers the complete 4-level appeal process with scripts.

## CANVA DESIGN NOTES

When designing this in Canva:
- Use the "eBook" or "Presentation (16:9)" template — both work well
- For an eBook: use portrait orientation (8.5" x 11")
- Color palette: Match your website (indigo/purple primary, amber accent)
- Each "program" gets its own page with:
  - Colored header bar (use brand purple)
  - Program number and title
  - Short intro paragraph
  - Key bullet points
  - "Your first step" box (colored callout)
- Use icons from Canva's free library (money bag for SSI, calendar for waiver, etc.)
- Keep text large enough to read on mobile (minimum 12pt body, 18pt headers)
- Total: 10–12 pages including cover and back cover
