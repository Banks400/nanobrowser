# LEGAL COMPLIANCE GUIDE
## What You Need to Know Before You Sell — And How to Stay Protected

This document outlines every legal risk area for this business, the realistic threat level for each, and exactly what to do. Read this before publishing anything.

---

## RISK 1: FTC ENDORSEMENT GUIDES (Testimonials & Claims)
**Threat Level: HIGH — FTC Actively Enforces This**

### The Rule
The FTC requires that:
- All testimonials must be genuine (from real customers)
- If testimonials describe results, you must disclose whether those results are typical
- Any material connection between you and the person endorsing (e.g., free product, payment) must be disclosed
- Earnings claims must be accompanied by a disclosure of typical results

### What This Means For You

**Testimonials:**
- The placeholder testimonials in the website MUST be replaced with real reviews from actual customers before launch
- When you collect real reviews, save screenshots or recordings as proof they are genuine
- Add near every results-based testimonial: *"Individual results will vary."*
- If you give someone a free guide in exchange for a review, they must disclose this

**Income/Results Claims in Marketing:**
- "families got $18,000 in ABA coverage" → Real result from a real customer. Fine to use WITH "individual results will vary"
- "paid for itself 100x over" → Specific enough to need backup. Only use if a real customer said this
- Vague motivational claims ("feel more confident," "feel prepared") are generally fine

**For your own marketing (e.g., "I make $X/month selling digital products"):**
- You cannot make income claims until you actually earn that income
- When you do share income, you must include a disclosure that results are not typical

### Action Items
- [ ] Replace all placeholder testimonials with real ones before launch
- [ ] Add "Individual results will vary" near all results-based testimonials
- [ ] Never create a fake review or fabricate results
- [ ] Save all customer testimonials (screenshots, emails) as documentation
- [ ] Reference: ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking

## RISK 2: UNAUTHORIZED PRACTICE OF LAW/MEDICINE (UPL/UPM)
**Threat Level: LOW-MEDIUM — Manageable With Right Framing**

### The Rule
Only licensed attorneys can give legal advice specific to an individual's situation. Only licensed medical professionals can give medical advice. Publishing general educational information is not practicing law or medicine — it's protected speech and publishing.

### Where the Line Is

**LEGAL (what your guides do):**
- "Under IDEA, parents have the right to request an independent educational evaluation"
- "SSI eligibility is determined by the Social Security Administration using these criteria..."
- "Research supports ABA therapy for autism (cite: American Academy of Pediatrics)"
- "Here is how the insurance appeal process generally works..."

**ILLEGAL (do not do this):**
- "Based on what you told me, your child definitely qualifies for SSI"
- "You should sue your school district"
- "Stop giving your child this medication"
- Providing specific legal strategy for an individual's lawsuit

### How Your Guides Stay Safe
- All content is framed as "how the system generally works" not "what you should do in your specific case"
- Every guide directs readers to consult professionals for their specific situation
- No guide tells a specific person that they qualify for a specific benefit

### Action Items
- [ ] Every guide must include the disclaimer on page 2 (already added to PDF generator)
- [ ] Avoid language like "you qualify" or "you should do X" — use "families may qualify" or "consider doing X"
- [ ] Always include "consult a qualified professional" recommendations throughout
- [ ] Do not respond to customer emails with specific legal/medical advice

## RISK 3: ACCURACY OF TIME-SENSITIVE INFORMATION
**Threat Level: MEDIUM — Ongoing Maintenance Required**

### The Problem
Government benefit amounts change annually. SSI amounts, SNAP income thresholds, CHIP eligibility levels, and program rules are updated regularly. If a guide published in 2025 still has 2023 dollar amounts in 2027, readers may act on wrong information.

### Solution: "As Of" Dating System

**In every guide:**
- Every dollar amount must be followed by: *(as of [year] — verify current amounts at [agency website])*
- Example: "SSI pays up to $943/month *(as of 2024 — verify current amount at SSA.gov)*"
- Every income threshold must have the same treatment

**Cover page/front matter of each guide:**
- Add: "Information in this guide is current as of [Month Year]. Program rules and benefit amounts change regularly. Always verify with the relevant agency before making decisions."

**Annual review process:**
- Set a calendar reminder every January to update all dollar figures
- SSI: SSA.gov announces new amounts in October each year
- SNAP: USDA announces new thresholds each October
- If you can't update immediately, add a note at the top: "Some figures may be out of date. Please verify."

### Action Items
- [ ] Add "as of [year]" qualifier to every dollar figure in all guides
- [ ] Add "current as of [date]" to the front matter of each guide
- [ ] Set annual January calendar reminder for content review
- [ ] Add to website footer: "Content reviewed [Month Year]. Program information changes frequently."

## RISK 4: EMAIL MARKETING COMPLIANCE (CAN-SPAM / GDPR)
**Threat Level: LOW-MEDIUM — Easy to Comply**

### CAN-SPAM (US Law)
Every marketing email must:
- Identify itself as an advertisement (the welcome email delivering a free guide is gray area, but nurture/sales emails must comply)
- Include your physical mailing address (use a PO box — get one at USPS.com for ~$10/month)
- Include a clear unsubscribe mechanism (MailerLite/ConvertKit handle this automatically)
- Honor unsubscribes within 10 business days (email platforms do this automatically)
- Not use deceptive subject lines

**Action items:**
- [ ] Get a PO box and add address to all email footers
- [ ] Ensure every email has an unsubscribe link (your email platform does this)
- [ ] Never use misleading subject lines ("Re: Your order" when there's no order)

### GDPR (If You Have EU Customers)
If anyone from the EU subscribes to your list:
- You need explicit consent to email them (double opt-in is safest)
- You must disclose what data you collect and how
- They have the right to have their data deleted

**Action items:**
- [ ] Enable double opt-in in your email platform settings
- [ ] Ensure Privacy Policy covers GDPR basics (the existing one does)
- [ ] Honor any "delete my data" requests within 30 days

## RISK 5: PRODUCT LIABILITY / CONSUMER PROTECTION
**Threat Level: LOW — Digital Products Have Inherently Low Exposure**

### The Rule
Digital information products have very low product liability exposure. You cannot be held liable for the content of educational information if:
- It is accurate to the best of your knowledge
- It includes appropriate disclaimers
- You are not holding yourself out as a licensed professional

### Protecting Yourself
- **Money-back guarantee:** A clear refund policy prevents chargebacks and most payment disputes. Chargebacks are far more damaging than refunds.
- **Terms of Service:** Limit liability to the purchase price ("Our maximum liability to you shall not exceed the amount you paid for this product")
- **No guaranteed results:** Never promise a specific financial outcome, IEP result, or legal outcome

## RISK 6: COPYRIGHT
**Threat Level: LOW — Properly Managed**

### What You Can Use Freely
- Government publications, laws (IDEA, ACA, SSA guidelines) — all public domain
- Statistics from government agencies (CDC autism prevalence, SSA benefit amounts)
- Information you have independently researched and written in your own words

### What You Cannot Use
- Copyrighted content from books, articles, or other guides without permission
- Images, graphics, or icons without appropriate licenses
- Canva templates are licensed for commercial use (check their license for your specific use)

### Action Items
- [ ] All written content in guides: original writing (already done) ✓
- [ ] Images in PDFs: use Canva's licensed assets or royalty-free sources (Unsplash, Pexels)
- [ ] If quoting research, cite the source (short quotes for educational purposes are generally fair use)
- [ ] Register copyright for each guide at copyright.gov ($65/filing) for major works

## RISK 7: BUSINESS STRUCTURE & TAXES
**Threat Level: MEDIUM if unaddressed — Straightforward to Fix**

### Form an LLC (Do This Before First Sale)
- Separates personal assets from business liability
- Cost: $50–$500 depending on state, filed with Secretary of State
- Most states: 1–2 week processing time
- Consider a registered agent service ($50–$150/year) to receive legal documents

### Get an EIN
- Free from IRS.gov
- Required to open a business bank account
- Takes 5 minutes online

### Open a Separate Business Bank Account
- Keeps business and personal finances separate (critical for taxes and liability protection)
- Required to maintain LLC protection in many states

### Taxes
- Digital product sales may be subject to sales tax in some states
- Several states (including Washington, Pennsylvania, Texas) tax digital downloads
- Use a sales tax automation tool (TaxJar or Avalara) once sales start — they calculate and remit automatically
- Estimated cost: $19–$50/month for automation, worth every penny to avoid state tax compliance headaches

### Action Items
- [ ] Form LLC before first sale
- [ ] Get EIN at IRS.gov (free)
- [ ] Open business checking account
- [ ] Set up TaxJar or Avalara once first sale comes in
- [ ] Consult CPA for tax structure advice (deductible as a business expense)

## RISK 8: WEBSITE ACCESSIBILITY (ADA)
**Threat Level: LOW-MEDIUM for Small Businesses**

The ADA requires websites to be accessible to people with disabilities. For a small business just starting, focus on basics:
- All images should have alt text
- Color contrast should meet WCAG 2.1 AA standards (your purple/white palette generally complies)
- Forms should be keyboard-navigable (yours are standard HTML)
- Add a basic accessibility statement to the website

## WHAT TO DO IF SOMEONE COMPLAINS OR THREATENS LEGAL ACTION

1. **Do not panic.** Most complaints from customers are resolved with a refund.
2. **Refund immediately** for any customer complaint — a $47 refund is infinitely preferable to a chargeback or legal dispute.
3. **Do not respond emotionally or make admissions** in writing if a legal threat is made.
4. **Consult an attorney** if a formal legal threat is received.
5. **Document everything** — keep records of all customer communications.

### Finding an Attorney (If Needed)
- Small business/LLC setup: any local business attorney, ~$300–$500 flat fee
- IP/copyright issues: intellectual property attorney
- FTC/advertising compliance: advertising attorney

This is a legitimate, legal business. Thousands of people publish guides on legal rights, government programs, and healthcare for profit — from books to courses to PDFs. The information in your guides is accurate and educational.

Your exposure is low if you:

2. Add accurate "as of [year]" dating to benefit amounts

4. Form an LLC before your first sale

None of this is complicated. The steps above take a total of a few hours. Do them before launch.
